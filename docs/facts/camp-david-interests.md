## Brief
- **Category:** history story + tactic explainer. The tactic is *Getting to Yes*'s "focus on interests, not positions", shown through the Camp David summit (Sept 5–17, 1978).
- **Hook idea (spoken line 1, 11 words):** "A peace deal was nearly lost. Eight photos helped save it."
  - The requested line, "A peace deal was saved by eight photos.", **claims too much**: Begin also accepted a revised US letter on Jerusalem that same day (see "Do not say"). "Helped save it" stays true.
  - **Hook title** (works on mute and adds to the spoken line): **"DAY 13. BAGS PACKED."**
  - **Frame-0 visual:** a suitcase snaps shut on a log-cabin porch. A hand fans out eight small framed photos on the table beside it. The inscriptions are illegible handwriting, never readable names.
- **Story arc (about 68 s):**
  1. **0–3 s, hook:** the suitcase, the eight photos, the title card.
  2. **3–10 s, setup:** September 1978, Camp David, the presidential retreat in Maryland. President Carter hosts Egypt's President Sadat and Israel's Prime Minister Begin. Israel has held Egypt's Sinai Peninsula since the 1967 war.
  3. **10–20 s, the positions:** Egypt wants all of the Sinai back. Israel wants to keep parts of it. A line on a brick map gets dragged back and forth like a tug-of-war, and every mile one side gains is a mile the other loses.
  4. **20–32 s, "Move one: ask WHY":** under each position is an interest. Egypt's is sovereignty: it's Egyptian land, and Egypt's flag should fly there. Israel's is security: no Egyptian tanks poised on its border. Two different needs, not one fight.
  5. **32–44 s, the option:** the framework returns the Sinai to full Egyptian sovereignty but limits the troops on it. Next to Israel's border, only UN forces and lightly armed police are allowed. *Getting to Yes* sums it up: the Egyptian flag would fly everywhere, but Egyptian tanks would be nowhere near Israel.
  6. **44–56 s, day 13, "Move two: talk to the person, not the position":** the talks nearly collapse over other issues (the final snag is a US letter on Jerusalem). Begin is reportedly ready to leave. He had asked for signed photos of the three leaders. Carter's staff quietly get the names of Begin's eight grandchildren, and Carter writes each photo to one grandchild by name. On Begin's cabin porch, Begin reads the names aloud and his eyes fill with tears. By Carter's account, they talk about grandchildren and war, and Begin agrees to try once more.
  7. **56–63 s, the payoff:** the Camp David Accords are signed that night at the White House. The peace treaty is signed on March 26, 1979, and Sadat and Begin share the 1978 Nobel Peace Prize. Israel completes its withdrawal from the Sinai in April 1982 (the tiny Taba strip only in 1989; leave that detail out or say "by 1982, the Sinai was back" carefully; see "Do not say").
  8. **63–68 s:** the takeaway, the soft CTA, then the end-card hold.
- **Tactics (1–2):**
  1. **Focus on interests, not positions** (*Getting to Yes*, Fisher & Ury, Harvard Negotiation Project). Positions are what people demand. Interests are why they demand it, and one interest can usually be met in several ways ("flag" vs. "tanks").
  2. **Separate the people from the problem** (also a *Getting to Yes* principle). The photos spoke to Begin as a grandfather, not as a negotiating position.
- **Takeaway line:** "Don't fight over what they demand. Find out why they want it." (Alternative: "Ask what they need, not what they demand.")

## Facts
- The Camp David summit was held **September 5–17, 1978**, at the presidential retreat in Maryland, with President Jimmy Carter, Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin. [State-OH]
- The three-way format soon broke down. Carter and Secretary of State Cyrus Vance met the Egyptian and Israeli delegations separately for most of the summit. [State-OH]
- US Middle East experts wrote a draft text that "would be revised numerous times during the Summit." [State-OH]
- The issues included the future of Israeli settlements and airbases in the Sinai, but the West Bank and Gaza were the hardest. [State-OH]
- Israel had occupied the Sinai Peninsula since the 1967 Six-Day War. [Britannica; Getting-to-Yes]
- In *Getting to Yes* (Fisher, Ury and Patton, Harvard Negotiation Project, first published 1981), the Sinai is the book's example of "focus on interests, not positions":
  - Egypt's position was that the whole Sinai be returned; Israel's was to keep part of it.
  - Israel's interest was security: it did not want Egyptian tanks poised on its border. Egypt's interest was sovereignty.
  - The book says that at Camp David, Sadat and Begin agreed to a plan that would return the Sinai to complete Egyptian sovereignty and, by demilitarizing large areas, still assure Israeli security. In the book's words: "The Egyptian flag would fly everywhere, but Egyptian tanks would be nowhere near Israel." [Getting-to-Yes]
- The signed *Framework for the Conclusion of a Peace Treaty between Egypt and Israel* agrees on:
  - "the full exercise of Egyptian sovereignty up to the internationally recognized border";
  - the withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from the Sinai;
  - the airfields Israel leaves behind to be used for civilian purposes only. [Avalon]
- Force limits in the Framework:
  - no more than one Egyptian division within about 50 km (30 mi) east of the Gulf of Suez and the Suez Canal;
  - in a strip west of the international border, 20–40 km (12–24 mi) wide, only UN forces and lightly armed civil police;
  - within 3 km east of the border, Israeli forces are limited to four infantry battalions plus UN observers. [Avalon]
- On September 15 Sadat reportedly told his delegation to pack and asked for his helicopter. Carter went to his cabin and persuaded him to stay. [Carter-Lib; Carter-KF]
- On the final day the talks nearly collapsed again. Begin reportedly told his delegation to pack their bags. A US letter on the status of Jerusalem was the last major obstacle. [Carter-KF; Wright; Time]
- **The photos (Carter's account, repeated by later histories):**
  - Begin had asked for signed photographs of the three leaders for his grandchildren.
  - Carter's secretary (named in accounts as Susan Clough) got the names of Begin's **eight** grandchildren, and Carter wrote each photo to one grandchild by name.
  - Carter took them to Begin, who was outside his cabin. Begin read the names aloud one by one, his lips trembled and his eyes filled with tears.
  - They talked about grandchildren and about war. Begin then agreed to keep trying. [Carter-KF; Wright; Eizenstat; Brookings]
- Carter also revised the Jerusalem letter, dropping the wording Begin objected to and referring instead to earlier US positions. Begin accepted the revised letter. [Carter-Lib; Wright]
- The summit produced **two framework documents** (one for an Egypt-Israel peace, one for Palestinian self-government in the West Bank and Gaza). They were signed at the White House on September 17, 1978, with Carter as witness. [State-OH; Avalon]
- The **Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty** was signed on **March 26, 1979**. [State-OH]
- Sadat and Begin shared the **1978 Nobel Peace Prize**. [Nobel]
- Israel completed its withdrawal from the Sinai on **April 25, 1982**. A small disputed strip at **Taba** was handed back only in **March 1989**, after international arbitration in 1988. [Britannica; ICCA/arbitration records; LA Times]

## Do not say
- **"The deal was saved by eight photos"** (only the photos). Begin also accepted Carter's revised Jerusalem letter. Say "helped save" or "helped turn it around".
- **Begin "walked out" or "left".** He didn't. Say he was reportedly ready to leave, or that his bags were reportedly packed.
- **A quote of Begin's exact words** (versions differ: "Let's try once more", "I'll make one last try", "Why don't we try once more?"). Paraphrase: "he agreed to try once more" (by Carter's account).
- **Legible grandchildren's names or exact inscriptions.** Accounts differ on the wording, and they are private people. Show illegible handwriting.
- **"The Sinai was completely demilitarized" / "zero tanks".** The deal limited forces by zone, and one Egyptian division was allowed near the Suez Canal. Say "largely demilitarized" or "limited forces", or use the *Getting to Yes* line, credited to the book.
- **"Every inch was returned in 1982."** Taba came back only in 1989.
- **The Sinai solution was invented on day 13, or by Carter alone, or by Fisher/Ury.** The arrangements were negotiated over the summit and finalized in the 1979 treaty. Roger Fisher's reported advice to the US team (the "one-text" procedure) and the number of drafts (often given as 23) were not verified, so leave them out.
- **The peace "has held ever since" / "still holds today".** Not re-verified for 2026, so leave it out.
- **Details of the Sinai settlements deal** (e.g. the Knesset vote on removing settlements) and **US airbase aid to Israel**: not verified in this pass, so leave them out.
- **Who came up with the photo idea.** Credit varies between Carter and his secretary, so say "Carter's staff" or "his secretary reportedly".
- **Any statistics about interest-based negotiation success rates.** None were verified, and none may be invented.
- **Real national emblems or logos in 3D:** flags are simplified and emblem-free, and "UN" appears only as generic "OBSERVERS" text.
