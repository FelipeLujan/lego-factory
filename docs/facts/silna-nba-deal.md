## Brief
- **Category:** History story (sports-business negotiation, 1976 to 2014).
- **Hook idea (spoken line 1, 11 words):** "This team played two seasons. Its owners made about $800 million."
  - Hook title (on mute; adds to the line, doesn't repeat it): **"NO TEAM. PAID FOREVER."**
  - Frame-0 visual: an empty brick arena with the lights going out, while a brick mailbox outside keeps filling with checks.
  - Note: the *owners* made the money, not the team. Keep "about": the $800M is the reported ~$300M plus the reported $500M.
- **Story arc (about 66 s):**
  1. **0–3 s hook:** the empty arena and the overflowing mailbox.
  2. **3–12 s, setup:** in 1974 two brothers from the textile business buy an ABA team and move it to St. Louis as the Spirits. Some nights only about 1,800 fans show up.
  3. **12–22 s, the squeeze (1976):** the ABA is collapsing. The NBA takes four ABA teams and leaves the Spirits and the Kentucky Colonels out. Kentucky's owner takes about $3 million and walks away.
  4. **22–36 s, "Move one":** the Silnas won't take a lump sum to go away. They ask for about $2.2M in cash plus **one-seventh of the four teams' national TV money, for as long as the NBA exists**. The four teams want into the NBA badly, so they sign. Back then the Finals aired on tape delay after the late news.
  5. **36–44 s, "Move two":** their lawyer, Donald Schupak, makes the definition of TV revenue deliberately broad so it "could not be evaded or made obsolete". No end date.
  6. **44–54 s, the payoff:** Magic, Bird and Jordan arrive and TV money explodes. The first check in 1980 is about half a million dollars, and by 2010–11 it's $17.5M a year. Total: about $300 million without ever paying a player.
  7. **54–60 s, the twist:** the brothers sue for a share of streaming and international money too. In 2014 the league and the four teams settle, reportedly paying **$500 million up front** to end most of the "forever" payments.
  8. **60–66 s, takeaway + soft CTA,** then the end-card hold.
- **Tactics (1–2):**
  1. **Trade a fixed price for a share of the upside.** Turn down a lump sum and take a percentage of something whose future value the other side underrates.

## Facts
- In 1974 brothers Ozzie and Daniel Silna, East Coast garment / textile businessmen, bought an ABA franchise (the Carolina Cougars) and moved it to St. Louis as the Spirits of St. Louis. [SI, Jon Wertheim, "Best sports deal ever? How the Silnas outsmarted the NBA", Apr 9 2014; Harvard PON, "When Negotiation Mistakes Compound over Time", Nov 15 2021]
- The Spirits played only two seasons, 1974–75 and 1975–76, at the St. Louis Arena. [SI 2014 ("lasted only two seasons"); Wikipedia "Spirits of St. Louis", citing Terry Pluto, *Loose Balls*, 1990]
- Attendance was poor. Play-by-play announcer Bob Costas recalled that even when stars like Dr. J came to town "there might be 1,800 fans." [SI 2014]
- Marvin Barnes, Caldwell Jones and Moses Malone played for the Spirits. [NYT, Richard Sandomir, Jan 7 2014 (Barnes, Jones); Wikipedia "Spirits of St. Louis" / Pluto, *Loose Balls* (Malone)]
- In 1976 only seven ABA teams were left. Four joined the NBA in the June 1976 merger: the Denver Nuggets, Indiana Pacers, New York Nets and San Antonio Spurs. The Virginia Squires folded. [SI 2014; NYT Jan 7 2014]
- The Spirits and the Kentucky Colonels were left out. Colonels owner John Y. Brown took a cash payout of about $3 million to fold his team (NYT: $3 million; SI: $3.3 million). [NYT Jan 7 2014; SI 2014]
- The Silnas were "unwilling to take a lump-sum payment to go away" [SI 2014]. Harvard PON says they "rejected a $3 million offer from the ABA" as compensation for folding. [Harvard PON 2021] → say "reportedly turned down a cash buyout".
- The deal: the four merging teams paid the Silnas a one-time payment of about $2.2 million (SI: $2.23M) plus **one-seventh of the national television revenue each of the four teams received, "as long as the league continued to exist"**, which amounted to payment in perpetuity. [NYT Jan 7 2014; SI 2014]
- The contract wording was "for as long as the NBA or its successors [continue] in its existence", and the rights were written as "visual media" rights. [Harvard PON 2021]
- According to Daniel Silna (as told to Forbes' Monte Burke), Ozzie suggested that any team left out should get a one-seventh share of the four admitted teams' TV money, and the Silnas were the only ones who made that demand. [Harvard PON 2021, citing Forbes] → hedge: "Daniel later said…"
- Lawyer Donald Schupak brokered the deal. He "inserted an intentionally broad definition of broadcast revenues". In a 2012 legal declaration he wrote: "I insisted instead that we add a new sentence [to] emphasize that this was a broad definition that could not be evaded or made obsolete." [SI 2014]
- The NBA's outside counsel then included a young lawyer named David Stern. [SI 2014]
- The four teams dealt directly with the brothers and were desperate to get into the NBA. The ABA's former general counsel, Michael Goldberg, said: "Schupak said they'd take TV rights in perpetuity as a kind of Hail Mary… What was missing was someone saying, 'Thirty years, 50 years, or until something happens, and it's over.'" [NYT Jan 7 2014]
- At the time, NBA TV money looked small: the ABA never had a national TV deal, and NBA Finals games aired on tape delay after the late local news. [SI 2014; Harvard PON 2021]
- Court documents obtained by SI show the brothers received $521,749 in 1980, the first year the contract vested. The payout passed $1 million a year by 1986–87 and $10 million by 1999–2000, and was $17.5 million in 2010–11. [SI 2014]
- TV money grew as the league boomed in the Magic Johnson / Larry Bird / Michael Jordan era. In 1997 the NBA signed a $2.6 billion deal with NBC and Turner, and in 2002 a $4.6 billion deal with ABC/ESPN and TNT. [Harvard PON 2021; NYT Jan 7 2014]
- By early 2014 the deal had paid the Silnas about $300 million. [NYT Jan 7 2014; SI 2014 ("more than $300 million cumulatively")]
- The NBA tried several times to buy them out, including before the 2008 financial crash. [NYT Jan 7 2014; Harvard PON 2021]
- In 2009 the Silnas sued in federal court, arguing they were also owed money from sources that didn't exist in 1976 (NBA TV, foreign broadcasts, League Pass). Judge Loretta A. Preska (U.S. District Court, Manhattan) encouraged a settlement. [SI 2014; NYT Jan 7 2014]
- January 7, 2014: the four former ABA teams (the Nets, Nuggets, Pacers and Spurs), the NBA and the Spirits of St. Louis Basketball Club, L.P. announced a conditional settlement of the federal lawsuit, subject to court approval. [NBA press release, pr.nba.com, Jan 7 2014 (archived)]
- The Silnas were to receive a **$500 million upfront payment**, financed through a private placement of notes by JPMorgan Chase and Merrill Lynch, "according to three people with direct knowledge". [NYT Jan 7 2014] SI's sources said the payment was "in excess of $500 million". The settlement terms are confidential. [SI 2014] → say "reportedly $500 million".
- The settlement ended the big perpetual payments, but the Silnas kept a smaller share of TV revenue through a new partnership with the four former ABA teams, which can buy them out later. [NYT Jan 7 2014; NBA press release Jan 7 2014]
- The ~$300M collected plus the reported $500M payment comes to about $800 million. [derived from NYT Jan 7 2014]
- Per the NYT's source, Daniel (then 69) was more reluctant than Ozzie (then 80) to keep fighting the league. [NYT Jan 7 2014] → hedge: "reportedly".
- The deal is "widely called the greatest in sports history". [NYT Jan 7 2014]
- ESPN's *30 for 30* documentary about the team, "Free Spirits", aired in October 2013. [SI 2014; Wikipedia "Spirits of St. Louis"]
- Ozzie Silna died on April 26, 2016, at 83. [NYT obituary, Richard Sandomir, Apr 28 2016]

## Do not say (UNSURE or disputed; left out)
- **"The NBA paid $500M"** as a flat statement. The money came through a settlement among the league, the four former ABA teams and the Spirits partnership, financed by new debt. Say "the league and the four teams settled, reportedly for $500 million up front." The NBA's press release gives no dollar figure.
- **"The team made $800M."** The owners made it. Keep "about", because both parts are reported estimates.
- **Totals "over $1 billion" / "close to $1 billion".** That's a projection (SI, IndyStar) including future partnership income, not a verified figure.
- **The exact purchase price of the team** ($1M per SI/PON vs $1.5M per Wikipedia / Pluto).
- **The Colonels' exact payout** ($3M per NYT vs $3.3M per SI). Say "about $3 million".
- **The exact $3 million offer the Silnas rejected.** Only Harvard PON gives the figure; if used at all, say "reportedly".
- **The first check:** PON says "about $200,000 in 1979", while SI (citing court documents) says $521,749 in 1980. Use SI's "about half a million in 1980" or leave it out.
- **The 1982 buyout attempt terms** (PON: $5M over five years vs an $8M-over-eight counter; Wikipedia: the reverse). Leave it out.
- **The "28-team minimum" clause / 1/49 share.** Only on Wikipedia (citing NBA.com); not independently verified.
- **The Virginia Squires origin story for the TV-share idea** (AP 2006): it conflicts with PON's version, so leave it out.
- **The "greatest deal known to man" quote** (attributed to a Knicks president): the speaker isn't verified.
- **The Silnas' Madoff losses:** documented, but off-topic and sensitive. Leave them out.
- **The 45 / 45 / 10 split with Schupak:** Wikipedia citing AP 2006; not independently checked. Leave it out.
- **No invented dialogue** between the brothers and NBA executives, and no real team or league logos in the 3D geometry.
