# voss-no-questions: Want a Yes? Ask for a No ("Is now a bad time?")

Format: `docs/DEALCALM-TOFU.md` (60–75 s, soft CTA, triple hook, "DEALCALM STORIES" stinger). Category: **tactic explainer** built on a real FBI anecdote that Voss tells in *Never Split the Difference* (ch. 4) and again in his company newsletter (Marti Evelsizer, FBI Pittsburgh), plus two dramatized everyday "Imagine…" scenes. I checked every claim with web search and source fetches on 2026-11-17. Saved to `docs/facts/voss-no-questions.md`.

**Caveat:** the Marti story comes **only from Voss** (the book, ch. 4, and his Black Swan Group newsletter of 16 Sep 2019). I found no independent report of the meeting itself, and no date is given. Her FBI career (special agent from 1995, Pittsburgh Field Office, Crisis Negotiation Team, retired 2016) is confirmed independently by her published obituary. The script must attribute the scene ("as Voss tells it"). I checked the book wording through Kindle highlights (The Modern Spartan book notes), the publisher's official sample (title, table of contents, copyright page), a Goodreads quote page, and a retelling that quotes the book (RiseWithDrew).

## Brief
- **Category:** Tactic explainer (No-oriented questions, ch. 4 "Beware 'Yes'—Master 'No'" of *Never Split the Difference*). It opens on a real FBI anecdote Voss tells and moves to two dramatized everyday scenes.
- **Hook idea (≤ 14 words, spoken line 1):** "Her boss wanted her off the FBI negotiation team. She made him say no." (14 words). Alternates: "Stop asking 'Got a minute?' Ask the question that makes people say no." (12) / "A good negotiator doesn't hunt for yes. They hunt for no." (11)
  - **Mute hook title** (adds to the line, doesn't repeat it): **WANT A YES? ASK FOR A NO.** (alt: **"NO" IS THE START**).
  - **Frame-0 visual:** a 1990s–2000s field-office supervisor's desk. A brick nameplate shaped like a team badge, reading **CRISIS NEGOTIATION COORDINATOR**, slides toward the supervisor's side of the desk. A big red brick **"NO"** sign drops into frame on the first spoken word and stops it.
- **Story arc (about 60–75 s):**
  1. **0–3 s, triple hook:** the badge sliding away, the "NO" brick, the hook line and the title.
  2. **3–14 s, the setup (attribute it):** As Voss tells it, Marti Evelsizer was the FBI's crisis negotiation coordinator in Pittsburgh, highly respected inside the Bureau and by the local police. The city police asked her to sit on the selection board for their hostage negotiators, picking her over her own boss. Her boss decides to take her off the role, saying she's neglecting her regular duties. (Voss says she wasn't.)
  3. **14–24 s, the move:** she doesn't argue and doesn't plead. She asks: **"Do you want the FBI to be embarrassed?"** He says **"No."** She asks: **"What do you want me to do?"** He leans back in his squeaky old chair: **"Look, you can keep the position… just don't let it interfere with your other duties."** She walks out with the role. Visual: the badge slides back to her side of the desk.
  4. **24–36 s, name it:** "That's a **No-oriented question**." Voss says pushing for "yes" puts people on guard, and an early "yes" is often **counterfeit** (said just to get rid of you). Saying "no" makes people **feel safe, secure and in control**. Once they're protected, they listen. Her "no" handed him control, and her next question let him decide her next move (the solution she wanted).
  5. **36–45 s, everyday #1 (Voss's own example):** "Do you have a few minutes to talk?" puts people on guard. Ask **"Is now a bad time to talk?"** Voss: either you get "Yes, it is a bad time" plus a better time (or a request to go away), or you get "No, it's not" **and total focus**.
  6. **45–60 s, everyday #2, "Imagine…" (dramatized):** a freelancer's client has gone quiet for three weeks. A pile of "Just checking in!" emails turns into a cobweb-covered brick mailbox. She sends six words: **"Have you given up on this project?"** The reply lands: "No! Budget got stuck with finance. Call Thursday?" Now they're negotiating again. (Fiction; the email line is Voss's.)
  7. **60–66 s, the rule:** keep your tone calm, not guilt-trippy. Use "Have you given up on…?" only for something they actually started (a Black Swan Group rule).
  8. **66–72 s, takeaway + soft CTA**, then the 2.4 s end hold.
- **Tactics (1–2):** **No-oriented questions** ("Is now a bad time to talk?", "Have you given up on this project?", "Are you against…?", "Is it a ridiculous idea…?"), plus a **follow-up "what/how" question** after the "no" ("What do you want me to do?").
- **Takeaway (one line):** "Stop chasing yes. Give them a safe no." (Alt: "'No' isn't the end. It's where the deal starts.")
- **Soft CTA line idea (≤ 8 words, spoken "Deal Calm"):** "Practice moves like this with Deal Calm." (Alt: "Rehearse your 'no' questions with Deal Calm.")

## Facts

**The source and the person**
- *Never Split the Difference: Negotiating As If Your Life Depended On It* by Chris Voss with Tahl Raz was first published in the US by Harper Business in 2016 (UK: Random House Business, 2016). Chapter 4 is titled **"Beware 'Yes'—Master 'No'"**, subtitled "How to Generate Momentum and Make It Safe to Reveal the Real Stakes". [Penguin Random House UK official sample PDF, copyright page and contents]
- Voss writes that he spent more than two decades in the FBI, fifteen years of it negotiating hostage situations, and that he was the Bureau's one **lead international kidnapping negotiator**. [*NSTD* ch. 1, official sample] His company says he held that role "prior to 2008" during a 24-year FBI career, and that before that he was lead crisis negotiator for the FBI's New York City division. [Black Swan Group, "Chris Voss" bio page]

**What Voss says about "No" and "Yes" (*NSTD* ch. 4, via Kindle highlights)**
- "Saying 'No' makes the speaker feel safe, secure, and in control, so trigger it." [*NSTD* ch. 4 key lessons, Kindle highlights (The Modern Spartan)]
- He writes that everyone is driven by "two primal urges: the need to feel safe and secure, and the need to feel in control," and that arguing people into a corner "is just going to push your counterpart to flee with a counterfeit 'Yes.'" [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights]
- He names three kinds of "yes": **Counterfeit, Confirmation and Commitment**. A counterfeit yes is one where the other side "plans on saying 'no' but either feels 'yes' is an easier escape route." Only a commitment yes "leads to action." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights]
- "'No' is not a failure… it really often just means 'Wait' or 'I'm not comfortable with that.'… It is not the end of the negotiation, but the beginning." [*NSTD* ch. 4 key lessons, Kindle highlights] He also writes: "Good negotiators welcome—even invite—a solid 'No' to start, as a sign that the other party is engaged and thinking." [same]
- His list of what "No" does: it "allows the real issues to be brought forth", "protects people from making—and lets them correct—ineffective decisions", "slows things down", "helps people feel safe, secure, emotionally comfortable, and in control of their decisions", and "moves everyone's efforts forward." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights]
- Voss credits Jim Camp's book ***Start with NO*** for the idea of giving the other side "the right to veto" from the outset. He writes that hostage negotiators had known this for years: "the quickest way to get a hostage-taker out was to take the time to talk them out, as opposed to 'demanding' their surrender." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights] *Start with No* by Jim Camp was published by Crown Business in 2002. [Open Library record]

**"Is now a bad time to talk?" (Voss's own example)**
- "If you're trying to sell something, don't start with 'Do you have a few minutes to talk?' Instead ask, 'Is now a bad time to talk?' Either you get 'Yes, it is a bad time' followed by a good time or a request to go away, or you get 'No, it's not' and total focus." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights; same wording on the Goodreads quote page] The chapter summary says "Is now a bad time to talk?" "is always better than 'Do you have a few minutes to talk?'" [*NSTD* ch. 4 key lessons]
- Voss's company lists it first of its "Top 4 No-Oriented Questions", replacing "Have you got a few minutes to talk?" / "Is now a good time to talk?". It says "People feel anxious when they sense you are seeking a yes" and "People feel safe and secure when they say no." [Chris Voss, "Negotiation Training: The Top 4 'No-Oriented' Questions™," Black Swan Group newsletter, 19 Dec 2023]
- The other three on that list: **"Is it a ridiculous idea…?"** (replacing "Is this a good idea?"), **"Are you against…?"** (replacing "Do you agree?"), and **"Have you given up on…?"** (replacing "Are you still interested in…?"). It notes you will often get "No, but…", and "the information you get after the 'but' is exactly what you need." [same newsletter]

**"Have you given up on this project?" (the ghosting email)**
- Voss: when someone ignores your email and your polite follow-up, "You provoke a 'No' with this one-sentence email. Have you given up on this project?" He says it "plays on your counterpart's natural human aversion to loss" and that the "No" answer "offers the other party the feeling of safety and the illusion of control while encouraging them to define their position and explain it to you." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights] In a passage quoted by a reviewer, he adds that it "makes the implicit threat that we will walk away on our own terms." [*NSTD* ch. 4, quoted in RiseWithDrew, "When 'going negative' works," 9 May 2023]
- The chapter summary: "If a potential business partner is ignoring you, contact them with a clear and concise 'No'-oriented question that suggests that you are ready to walk away. 'Have you given up on this project?' works wonders." [*NSTD* ch. 4 key lessons, Kindle highlights]
- The Black Swan Group's rule of use: "Someone can't give up on something they haven't started." It isn't a cold opener. [Black Swan Group newsletter, 19 Dec 2023] Voss's Oct 2025 newsletter calls it "The Magic Label". It says "'No' feels safe. 'No' means they're in control." Its sequence: ask, then "Shut up. Give them 6-10 seconds of silence," then mirror and label. [Chris Voss, "The Magic Label: 'Have You Given Up On _____?'," Black Swan Group newsletter, 23 Oct 2025]

**The documented case: Marti Evelsizer, FBI Pittsburgh (Voss's account)**
- Mary "Marti" Kain Evelsizer was hired as an FBI special agent in **1995**, assigned to the **Pittsburgh Field Office**, and was a member of the FBI's **Crisis Negotiation Team**, training law-enforcement officers in western Pennsylvania, international agencies, and at the FBI Academy. She retired from the FBI in **2016**. [Obituary, Devlin Funeral Home of Cranberry, 2021] *(Independent confirmation of her role.)*
- Voss calls her "the FBI's Crisis Negotiation Coordinator in Pittsburgh" and "a dynamo and a negotiating genius, which earned her huge respect both within the Bureau and with the local police." The city police department's hostage-negotiation team asked her to sit on its selection board for new candidates. "Picking her, and doing so over her boss, was an unprecedented move." [*NSTD* ch. 4, quoted in RiseWithDrew, 9 May 2023]
- Her immediate boss "was jealous of her phenomenal relationship with the Pittsburgh Police Department." He decided to remove her from the negotiation team, "with the proffered excuse that she was neglecting her investigations (she wasn't)." [Chris Voss, "How Expert Negotiators Use 'No' to Close," Black Swan Group newsletter, 16 Sep 2019 (notes "This story is also in Never Split the Difference")]
- The exchange: **"Do you want the FBI to be embarrassed?" she said. "No," he answered. "What do you want me to do?" she responded.** He "leaned back in his chair, one of those 1950s faux-leather numbers that squeak meaningfully… He was in control. 'Look, you can keep the position,' he said. 'Just go back out there and don't let it interfere with your other duties.' And a minute later Marti walked out with her job intact." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights; corroborated in Black Swan newsletter, 16 Sep 2019 ("She retained her position")]
- Voss's reading: "By pushing for a 'No,' Marti nudged her supervisor into a zone where he was making the decisions. And then she furthered his feelings of safety and power with a question inviting him to define her next move." [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights]
- **No date** for the meeting is given in either source. It must fall between 1995 (when she joined) and the book's 2016 publication.

**Optional extra beats (Voss's own accounts; attribute them if used)**
- **Ch. 4 opening (Voss's own "no"):** Voss writes that when he asked "Amy", who ran the FBI's New York crisis-negotiation team, how to become a hostage negotiator, she told him "No" and to go away, then said he could volunteer at a suicide hotline: "No guarantees." He writes that her "No's" were "just the gateway to 'Yes.'" [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights] *(Sensitive topic for TikTok; see Do not say.)*
- **Fundraising scripts:** Voss writes that "'Yes pattern' scripts had been giving poor rates of return for years. All the steps were 'Yes,' but the final answer was invariably 'No.'" Then his student Ben (named Ben Ottenhoff in book summaries) read *Start with NO* in Voss's class and tested a "No"-oriented script. [*NSTD* ch. 4, Kindle highlights] *(For the result figure, see Do not say.)*
- **Herjavec email (Voss's company account):** the Black Swan Group says Voss closed a ticket deal with *Shark Tank*'s Robert Herjavec by emailing two No-oriented questions ("Are you against making a firm commitment right now…?" / "Is it ridiculous to ask…?") and got a commitment one minute later. [Black Swan Group newsletter, 16 Sep 2019] *(Self-reported marketing anecdote. Optional; don't use it as proof.)*

**Related research (optional; I don't recommend it as a proof beat)**
- **Guéguen & Pascual (2000):** in a mall in France, a young confederate asked 80 random adults for coins for the bus. With the added line "But you are free to accept or to refuse," **47.5%** complied, vs **10.0%** without it. [Guéguen & Pascual, "Evocation of Freedom and Compliance: The 'But You Are Free of…' Technique," *Current Research in Social Psychology* 5(18), 2000, paper text]
- A meta-analysis supported the "but you are free" effect. [Carpenter, *Communication Studies* 64(1), 6–17, 2013, DOI 10.1080/10510974.2012.727941] **But** a 2023 pre-registered meta-analysis (52 experiments, N = 19,528) found a medium overall effect (g = 0.44) and **no effect** among the seven low-risk-of-bias studies, with critically low reproducibility. [Fillon, Souchet, Pascual & Girandola, *Meta-Psychology* 7, 2023, abstract via Crossref]
- **Fit:** this research is about *granting freedom to refuse*, not about No-oriented questions as such. If you use it at all, say "related research on letting people refuse", give the replication caveat, and never say "science proves 'no' questions work".

**The everyday examples (dramatized; frame them as "Imagine…")**
- The cold-call scene uses Voss's own contrast ("Do you have a few minutes to talk?" vs "Is now a bad time to talk?"). The callee's reaction is illustrative.
- The freelancer/ghosting scene is **illustrative fiction**. Only the line "Have you given up on this project?" is Voss's. The 21-day silence, the client's reply ("No! Budget got stuck… Call Thursday?") and the timing are invented for illustration. Don't present them as typical results.

## Do not say
- **"A 'no' script raised donations 23%" / "23 percent better rate of return."** This is Voss's account of a student's fundraising test (book, ch. 4, and podcast retellings). It's not published research and not independently verified. Left out.
- **"It works every time" / "they will respond quickly" as a promise.** The Black Swan newsletters say "they will respond quickly". That's Voss's marketing claim, not a measured result. Left out.
- **"Science proves No-oriented questions work."** No peer-reviewed study tests Voss's No-oriented questions directly. The nearest research ("but you are free") is related and has replication problems (see above).
- **"People fear loss exactly twice as much as they value gains"** (as in the 2025 newsletter). Say "people hate losing what they've started", or leave it out. Don't state a precise multiplier as Voss's finding.
- **"The nine-word email."** One search summary called it that, but the line "Have you given up on this project?" is six words. Voss calls it a "one-sentence email".
- **"Marti was being fired / demoted / she saved her career."** Per Voss, her boss was removing her from the **crisis-negotiation coordinator role / negotiation team**, not firing her. Say "take her off the negotiation team" and "she kept the position".
- **"She also got the seat on the selection board."** A retelling (RiseWithDrew) adds this, but the book excerpt I checked only says she kept her position. Left out.
- **The supervisor's name or likeness, and calling him anything beyond Voss's account.** He's unnamed; describe him only as Voss does ("jealous", per Voss).
- **A year for the Marti meeting**, or saying Voss was in the room. Neither is stated. Say "as Voss tells it".
- **Her death (2021) or any personal details from the obituary** beyond her FBI role. Not needed, and keep it respectful.
- **Marti's exact quote shortened to "Do you want the FBI embarrassed?"** That's one retelling's wording. Use the book's: "Do you want the FBI to be embarrassed?"
- **"The FBI officially teaches 'Is now a bad time?'"** That's Voss's / the Black Swan Group's sales advice, not FBI doctrine. Attribute it to Voss.
- **The "suicide hotline" detail from the Amy story** as a hook or on-screen text. It's a sensitive topic on TikTok; skip it or say "she told him to go get experience".
- **Neuroscience claims** ("'No' calms the amygdala", "dopamine"). Not sourced. Left out.
- **"Never ask yes questions."** Voss says "Yes" is the final goal, just "don't aim for it at the start". Don't overstate it.
- **Logos and likenesses:** no FBI seal, Pittsburgh Police patch, book-cover art, Black Swan logo, or Voss/Evelsizer likenesses. Use neutral minifigs and generic signage (AGENT, SUPERVISOR, POLICE, NEGOTIATOR SELECTION BOARD).
