# sony-marvel-25m: The $25 million bundle Sony turned down

## Brief

- **Hook idea (≤ 14 words, spoken line 1):** "Sony could have bought Iron Man, Thor and Black Panther for $25 million." (13 words). Hook title (on mute, adds rather than repeats): **"THEY ONLY WANTED ONE HERO"**. Frame 0: a brick price tag reading "$25M" hanging on a long shelf of hero-silhouette boxes, then one box being pulled out.

  3. 12–24 s, the counteroffer: Marvel (led by Ike Perlmutter, according to Fritz's book) puts nearly the whole roster on the table: Iron Man, Thor, Ant-Man, Black Panther and more, for $25 million.

## Facts
- In 1998 Marvel, fresh out of bankruptcy and short of cash, was negotiating with Sony over the movie rights to Spider-Man. [Ben Fritz, *The Big Picture: The Fight for the Future of Movies* (2018), via Quartz (Jan 2018), IGN, Gizmodo]
- The negotiator for Sony was a young executive, Yair Landau. [Fritz via Quartz/IGN] Landau later became vice chairman of Sony Pictures Entertainment and president of Sony Pictures Digital. [DLD conference bio; Screen Daily]
- Marvel (under Ike Perlmutter, per IGN's account of the book) offered Sony the film rights to nearly all its other characters, including Iron Man, Thor, Ant-Man and Black Panther, for $25 million. [Fritz via Quartz/IGN/Gizmodo] Attribute it: "according to Ben Fritz's book".
- Landau took the offer to his bosses. As he recalled it, the answer was: "Nobody gives a s--- about any of the other Marvel characters. Go back and do a deal for only Spider-Man." [Fritz via Quartz/Gizmodo] In the video, use a clean paraphrase: "Nobody cares about the other characters. Just get Spider-Man."
- Sony did a deal for Spider-Man alone. [Fritz via Quartz]
- Marvel came out of Chapter 11 in October 1998, after merging with Toy Biz (Perlmutter and Avi Arad's company), as Marvel Enterprises. [Encyclopedia.com company history; Wikipedia, Toy Biz]
- Sam Raimi's *Spider-Man* (Sony/Columbia, May 2002) opened to about $114.8M in the U.S., the first $100M+ opening weekend, and grossed about $821M worldwide. [Box Office Mojo via Wikipedia]
- In 2005 Marvel arranged a $525 million film-financing facility through Merrill Lynch, using film rights to some of its characters as collateral, so it could make its own movies. [Marvel SEC filing (2005); The Guardian]
- *Iron Man* (2008), Marvel Studios' first self-financed film, launched the Marvel Cinematic Universe and grossed about $585M worldwide. [Forbes; Box Office Mojo]
- Disney announced in August 2009 that it would buy Marvel Entertainment for about $4 billion, and the deal closed at the end of 2009. [Disney announcement; widely reported]
- *Avengers: Endgame* (2019) grossed about $2.8 billion worldwide and briefly became the highest-grossing film ever. [Box Office Mojo / The Numbers; Disney]
- The MCU is the highest-grossing film franchise. Say "tens of billions of dollars" rather than an exact total. [Wikipedia / The Numbers]

## Do not say
- **"Sony could have owned the Avengers"** as stated: the Hulk's film rights were at Universal, and the rights to several characters (Iron Man too) moved between studios around that time. Say "Iron Man, Thor, Black Panther" or "most of the future Avengers", always "according to the book".
- **Captain America by name in the offer:** the published accounts list Iron Man, Thor, Ant-Man and Black Panther ("and others"). I could not confirm that Captain America was named, so leave him out (or say "and more").
- **The exact price Sony paid for Spider-Man** ($7M vs. $10M are both reported), and the backend terms (a reported 5% of gross and a 50/50 consumer-products split): the reports conflict. Say only "a deal for Spider-Man alone".
- **Who exactly said the quote** (the names of Landau's bosses): it isn't named in the coverage, so keep them unnamed.
- **That Sony "laughed" or that it was a single meeting:** neither is in the sources.
- **The exact MCU total** ($30B+ vs. $35B+ changes with each release): say "tens of billions".
- **Any per-character value or "Sony lost $X billion" figure:** it would be invented.
- **That Sony held the Spider-Man home-video/DVD rights before 1998:** some outlets say so, but it isn't verified here.
- **The exact date or month of the 1998 offer:** not given in the coverage.
