## Facts
- Van Halen's 1982 world-tour contract rider, published by The Smoking Gun, lists under its "Munchies" section: "M & M's (WARNING: ABSOLUTELY NO BROWN ONES)". [Snopes, "Did Van Halen's Concert Contract Require the Removal of Brown M&Ms?", D. Mikkelson, 2001, reproducing the 1982 rider; The Pueblo Chieftain, "Rock scars", A. Matthew, 28 Mar 2010]
- The rider was reportedly a typewritten document of about 53 pages. Say "dozens of pages" or "thick as a phone book" instead of an exact count. [The Smoking Gun via Van Halen News Desk (fan site); Roth said the rider "read like a version of the Chinese Yellow Pages": *Crazy From the Heat*, Hyperion 1997, pp. 97–98, quoted by Snopes]
- The Pueblo Chieftain (2010) reported that the M&M demand appeared in the rider for all Van Halen shows at that time. [Pueblo Chieftain, 2010]
- In his memoir *Crazy From the Heat* (1997), David Lee Roth wrote that "Van Halen was the first band to take huge productions into tertiary, third-level markets. We'd pull up with nine eighteen-wheeler trucks, full of gear, where the standard was three trucks, max." [Roth, *Crazy From the Heat*, pp. 97–98, via Snopes]
- Roth wrote that there were "many, many technical errors — whether it was the girders couldn't support the weight, or the flooring would sink in, or the doors weren't big enough to move the gear through." [same]
- Roth said the clause was "a little test" placed in the technical part of the rider, among items like power-socket specs. As he recalled it: "article number 126, in the middle of nowhere, was: 'There will be no brown M&M's in the backstage area, upon pain of forfeiture of the show, with full compensation.'" Present the article number and the penalty as Roth's recollection. [same]
- Roth: "So, when I would walk backstage, if I saw a brown M&M in that bowl ... well, line-check the entire production. Guaranteed you're going to arrive at a technical error. They didn't read the contract." He added that sometimes the error was "literally, life-threatening." [same]
- The band's manager, Noel Monk, has also described the clause as a test of whether promoters read the rider. [Wikipedia "Van Halen", Contract riders section, citing Roth and Monk; Monk's memoir *Runnin' with the Devil* (2017)]
- Pueblo, Colorado: on March 30, 1980, Van Halen played Massari Gym at the University of Southern Colorado (now Colorado State University Pueblo), promoted by Barry Fey's Feyline. About 5,000 people attended. [Pueblo Chieftain, 2010; Wikipedia "Barry Fey"]
- According to the student paper USC Today (as reported by the Chieftain), the caterer saw the M&M demand but refused to comply. The band smashed candies into the carpet, and a food fight continued after the show. [Pueblo Chieftain, 2010]
- The gym's new floor was damaged under the stage. Van Halen blamed the school for not knowing how much the staging weighed. [Pueblo Chieftain, 2010]
- Roth's version: the university "took the contract rather kinda casual… They hadn't read the contract," and "the staging sank through their floor", a new "rubberized bouncy basketball flooring". He said the press then reported that he "discovered brown M&M's and did eighty-five thousand dollars' worth of damage to the backstage area. Well, who am I to get in the way of a good rumor?" [Roth, *Crazy From the Heat*, via Snopes]
- Contemporary press framed it as a candy tantrum. A radio item quoted by Snopes spoke of an "M&M riot" in which the band did "thousands of dollars of damage" over brown M&M's. Note: that item wrongly placed it in New Mexico. [Snopes, citing the Washington Post, 28 Jul 1981]
- Days after the show the university announced a ban on such concerts. It lasted until that October. [Pueblo Chieftain, 2010]
- Snopes rates the overall legend "Mixture": the clause was real, but the popular "pure diva" motive was not. [Snopes]
- Surgeon Atul Gawande retold the brown-M&M story in *The Checklist Manifesto* (2009) as an example of verifying complex work. Optional line. [multiple summaries of *The Checklist Manifesto*]

## Do not say
- **Exact damage dollar figures** as fact (Roth's "$12,000" dressing room and "$80,000" floor, the press's "$85,000"). The Chieftain reports the university put room damage at **$3,500**, floor estimates ranged "as high as $80,000", and band representatives said about $12,000. If a number is used, say "Roth claimed…". Better to skip numbers.
- That the brown M&M **at Pueblo directly caused** the band to find the floor problem, or that the check "saved lives" there. Roth links the two. A student stage-crew member (Larry Frazier) disputed the logic: different groups handle catering and staging, and Feyline's crew insisted on plywood pads under the stage legs.
- That the actual 1982 rider text calls it "Article 126" or contains a "forfeiture with full compensation" penalty. The published rider shows it under "Munchies". The article number and penalty come from Roth's memory. Attribute them: "Roth said…".
- That the band ever actually **cancelled a show** over a brown M&M. Not documented.
- That Roth personally trashed the room "in a Shakespearean rage" as established fact. That is his own colourful telling. The Chieftain describes a band-wide food fight, which differs.
- Roth's "850 lights" figure (from a 2012 video): not confirmed from a primary source.
- Who wrote the clause (Roth vs. Monk vs. crew): unclear, so leave it out.
- That the venue was in New Mexico (an old radio error), or calling it a "riot".
- Any M&M's / Mars branding or Van Halen logo on screen. Use generic coloured candy studs.
- Any invented statistics about how often contracts go unread. If needed, use a dramatized "Imagine…" example (e.g., a freelancer adding one small, harmless checkbox to see if a client read the terms).
