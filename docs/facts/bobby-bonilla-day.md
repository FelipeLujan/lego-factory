## Facts
- Bobby Bonilla was born February 23, 1963. He played in MLB from 1986 to 2001 and was a 6-time All-Star. [Wikipedia "Bobby Bonilla", citing People 1988 / Pirates media guide]
- He signed with the Mets in the 1991–92 offseason and became the highest-paid player in baseball at the time. ESPN describes it as a $29 million deal that ran 1992–1996. [Wikipedia "Bobby Bonilla"; ESPN, Darren Rovell, Jul 1 2016]
- He came back to the Mets before the 1999 season. In 1999 he hit .160 and argued with manager Bobby Valentine. [Chicago Tribune/AP, Jan 4 2000; Wikipedia]
- The Mets owed him **$5.9 million** for 2000, the final season of his contract. He was placed on unconditional-release waivers on Monday, January 3, 2000, after agreeing to defer the salary. It compounds at **8% a year**. [Chicago Tribune/AP, "Mets Say Goodbye To Bonilla, Eat $29 Million", Jan 4 2000]
- GM Steve Phillips: "It was a distraction and a problem we needed to address this off-season." [Chicago Tribune/AP, Jan 4 2000]
- Under the revised contract he gets **25 equal payments of $1,193,248.20, every July 1 from 2011 to 2035**, a total of **$29,831,205**. [Chicago Tribune/AP, Jan 4 2000; ESPN 2016 and 2021]
- The math checks out: $5.9M compounded at 8% for 10 years is about $12.74M. Paid out as 25 yearly payments at 8%, that is exactly $1,193,248.20 a year. [derived; reproduces the AP figure] → on screen, "about five times the original" ($29.8M ÷ $5.9M ≈ 5.06).
- ESPN says Bonilla's agent, **Dennis Gilbert**, was also an insurance agent, so he was "more uniquely prepared to understand annuity-type payouts than other agents". The club "negotiated with Gilbert to attach an 8 percent annual interest rate". [ESPN, Rovell, Jul 1 2016] (See "Do not say" about naming the agent.)
- Bret Saberhagen, also a Gilbert client, gets $250,000 a year from the Mets for 25 years starting in 2004. ESPN calls it "the inspiration for Bonilla's deal". [ESPN 2016; ESPN, Dan Mullen, Jul 1 2021]
- The Mets acquired pitcher Mike Hampton from Houston on December 23, 1999, just before releasing Bonilla. ESPN notes Hampton's salary was "conveniently $5.75 million" and frames the deferral as money the Mets could spend elsewhere. [Wikipedia "2000 New York Mets season", citing Baseball-Reference; ESPN 2016]
- The 2000 Mets won the NL pennant (Hampton was NLCS MVP) and lost the World Series to the Yankees, 4 games to 1. [Wikipedia "2000 World Series" / "Mike Hampton"]
- After the Mets, Bonilla played 2000 for the Braves and 2001 for the Cardinals. His last game was October 7, 2001, and he retired after that season. [Wikipedia, citing Jet, Mar 18 2002]
- **Madoff link (keep "reportedly"):** ESPN: "it is well known that their owners, the Wilpons, had many accounts with investor Bernie Madoff", and deferring was attractive because the interest rate "would be lower than the money they were (fictionally) getting back from Madoff". "The Mets have never really talked about the deal." [ESPN 2016] ESPN 2021: ownership "was invested in a Bernie Madoff account that promised double-digit returns". [ESPN 2021]
- **The court-record version:** in pre-trial filings, Madoff trustee Irving Picard said Wilpon and Katz expected "steady, 10 to 14 percent returns" and "began to budget them into their business plans". The Mets' controller, Len Labita, described a fund set up with Madoff to invest the deferred salaries owed to Darryl Strawberry, **Bobby Bonilla**, Roger Cedeño and Cliff Floyd: "if you're paying somebody 8 percent of interest and you're able to earn 10, you're going to make 2 percent on the money every year." The owners' lawyers called the trustee's portrayal a "complete distortion" and said the team paid players with its own money. [NYT, Richard Sandomir, "Trustee Says Mets Saw Madoff as House Money", Feb 21 2012]
- Madoff was arrested in 2008 for what was "probably history's greatest Ponzi scheme". [NYT Feb 21 2012]
- In March 2012 Wilpon and Katz agreed to settle Picard's lawsuit for $162 million. [NYT, Sandomir & Belson, Mar 19 2012, as cited in Wikipedia "Fred Wilpon"]
- July 1 is known among fans as **"Bobby Bonilla Day"**. Steve Cohen bought the team in November 2020 and soon after mentioned possibly celebrating Bonilla at Citi Field every year. In 2021 the Mets were "embracing Bonilla's day". [ESPN 2021]
- He'll be paid through 2035, when he'll be 72. [ESPN 2021]
- ESPN's own analysis: if Bonilla had taken the $5.9M in 2000 and invested it 60/40 in stocks and bonds, he'd have had about $16.5M by end-2015. But if he needed to live off it from 2011, the guaranteed deal comes out ahead. [ESPN 2016] (Nuance; only use it if the script needs a "was it really a steal?" beat.)

- **"The Mets did it because of Madoff"** as a flat fact. It's reported (ESPN) and alleged in the trustee's filings (NYT), and the owners disputed the trustee's portrayal. Say "reportedly expected about 10% from Madoff".
