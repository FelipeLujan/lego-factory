## Facts
**The flight**
- On **November 24, 1971 (Thanksgiving Eve)**, a man bought a one-way ticket with cash at Portland International Airport for **Northwest Orient Airlines Flight 305** to Seattle–Tacoma, about a 30-minute flight. [FBI famous-case page via search summary; Britannica; Wikipedia "D. B. Cooper"]
- He used the name **"Dan Cooper"**. The press later got it wrong as **"D. B. Cooper"** (Oregon Journal reporter James Long's deadline error, picked up by UPI), and the name stuck. [Wikipedia; FBI via search summary]
- Eyewitnesses described a white man in his **mid-40s**, dark hair, in a dark business suit, white shirt, **thin black tie** and black raincoat, carrying a **black attaché case**. [Wikipedia, citing FBI files; FBI via search summary: "mid-40s… business suit, white shirt, black tie"]
- The plane was a **Boeing 727-100**. He sat in the **last row** and ordered a **bourbon and 7-Up**. [Wikipedia] (Seat is given as 18-E or 18C depending on the source, so say "the last row".)
- Six crew were aboard: Captain William Scott, First Officer William Rataczak, Flight Engineer Harold Anderson, and flight attendants Alice Hancock, **Tina Mucklow** and **Florence Schaffner**. The flight left Portland on schedule at **2:50 pm**. [Wikipedia]

**The note and the "bomb"**
- Shortly after takeoff he handed Schaffner a note. Assuming it was a businessman's phone number, she reportedly put it in her purse unread. He leaned over and said, **"Miss, you'd better look at that note. I have a bomb."** [Wikipedia; widely reported] (Keep the "reportedly" for the purse detail.)
- He opened the attaché case to show what appeared to be a bomb: wires, a large battery and **red cylinders** (sticks). [FBI via search summary: "a mass of red sticks and wires"; Wikipedia] Say "what looked like a bomb". It was never confirmed real or fake.
- He later took the note back. [Wikipedia; Crime Museum via search summary]

**The demands (exact)**
- The captain relayed them: **"$200,000 in a knapsack by 5:00 pm… two front parachutes, two back parachutes… the money in negotiable American currency."** [Wikipedia, quoting the crew's relay]
- He asked for the money in **$20 bills**. [FBI via search summary: "$200,000 in twenty-dollar bills and four parachutes"; Britannica]
- Through Mucklow he added more conditions: **fuel trucks** had to meet the plane in Seattle; passengers stayed seated while the money came aboard; he'd release them once he had it; the parachutes would come aboard last. [Wikipedia]
- **Calm demeanour:** Mucklow later said he "was not nervous. He seemed rather nice and he was not cruel or nasty." [Wikipedia, citing her account; Portland Monthly via search summary]

**The standoff and the trade**
- Passengers were told the delay was a "minor mechanical difficulty". The plane **circled Puget Sound for about two hours** while police and the FBI assembled the money and parachutes. [Wikipedia]
- **Northwest president Donald Nyrop authorised paying the ransom** and told employees to cooperate. [Wikipedia; search summaries]
- The ransom came from **Seattle First National Bank**: **10,000 unmarked $20 bills** in a bag of about **19 lb (8.5 kg)**. The FBI **photographed the serial numbers on microfilm**. [Wikipedia]
- Seattle police got the two front (reserve) parachutes from a local skydiving school and the two back (main) parachutes from a local pilot. [Wikipedia]
- At Sea-Tac, he had the plane parked away from the terminal. **One airline courier** brought the money and parachutes. Mucklow carried the money bag back to him, and then he **released the passengers**. [Wikipedia; FBI via search summary: "exchanged the 36 passengers" for the money and parachutes; Britannica: "released all 36 passengers"]
- He cut the canopy out of one reserve parachute with a pocket knife and stuffed some of the money into the empty container (he complained the money came in a cloth bag, not a knapsack). [Wikipedia, citing Mucklow]

**The escape**
- New orders: fly toward **Mexico City** at the slowest speed possible without stalling (about 100 knots), **no higher than 10,000 ft**, landing gear down, flaps at **15°**, cabin **unpressurised**. They agreed on a refuelling stop in **Reno**. [Wikipedia; Britannica via search summary]
- The plane took off **around 7:40 pm** with Cooper, Mucklow, and the three pilots and engineer aboard. [Wikipedia]
- Mucklow was sent to the cockpit and was the last person to see him, tying what looked like the money bag around his waist. **Around 8:00 pm** a cockpit light showed the **aft airstair** had been lowered. At **about 8:13 pm** the tail suddenly pitched upward, which is taken as the likely moment of the jump. [Wikipedia, citing FBI interviews]
- The plane landed in Reno with the airstair still down, and **Cooper was gone**. [Wikipedia; FBI via search summary] (Reno landing time differs between accounts, so don't give it.)
- Agents recovered his **black clip-on tie** and tie clip and **two of the four parachutes**. [Wikipedia]

**Aftermath**
- **February 10, 1980:** 8-year-old **Brian Ingram** found three decaying packets of ransom $20s on the Columbia River shore at Tena (Tina) Bar, downstream from Vancouver, Washington. The serial numbers matched. The total is usually given as **$5,800** (some sources say about $6,000). [FBI/Britannica via search summaries; Wikipedia; KIRO 7] **Say "about $6,000" or "$5,800". The rest of the money has never been found.**
- **July 2016:** the FBI suspended active investigation of the case (NORJAK) to focus resources elsewhere. It still accepts physical evidence related to the parachutes or the money. [FBI via search summary; Wikipedia: announced July 8, 2016]
- His identity and fate are **still unknown**, and the FBI has never stated an official position on whether he survived. [Wikipedia; FBI via search summary]
- After the 1972 copycat hijackings, the FAA required Boeing 727s to have a spring-loaded device (later called the **"Cooper vane"**) that stops the aft airstair being lowered in flight. [Wikipedia; FlightAware via search summary]
- It is often described as the only unsolved hijacking in US commercial aviation history. [Wikipedia: "the only documented unsolved case of air piracy in the history of commercial aviation"; Britannica/others] (Use "often called".)

**Hedged interpretation (only with a hedge, or cut)**
- **Why four parachutes?** One widely repeated theory: asking for two sets implied he might take a hostage, which discouraged anyone from handing over sabotaged gear. [Wikipedia states it as an inference; otherwise forums and popular articles] **Say it only as "One theory…" / "Some think…" + "nobody knows for sure."**

## Do not say
- **"He negotiated $200,000 out of the FBI."** Wrong: the airline (Northwest's president) authorised the ransom and a Seattle bank supplied the cash. The FBI recorded the serial numbers.
- **As fact: "He asked for four parachutes so nobody could hand him a rigged one."** It's speculation (the hostage-implication theory). Hedge it or cut it.
- **"The bomb was fake" / "the bomb was real."** Never established. Say "what looked like a bomb".
- **Who he was:** any suspect (Richard McCoy, Ted Braden, Robert Rackstraw, Kenneth Christiansen, etc.). None was confirmed. Leave them out.
- **"He died in the jump" / "he got away with it."** Unknown. Agents disagree, and the FBI has no official position.
- **Claims that police found the dummy reserve, or that he rejected it.** The reports conflict. (One reserve was reportedly a sewn-shut training chute and was never found.) Leave it out.
- **Exact landing times** in Seattle (5:39 vs 5:46 pm) or Reno (≈10:15 vs 11:02 pm), and **seat 18C vs 18E**: the sources disagree.
- **The note's exact full wording:** versions differ. Use only the spoken line "Miss, you'd better look at that note. I have a bomb."
- **Passenger count, if precision matters:** the FBI and Britannica say he released "36 passengers". Wikipedia counts 36 *including* Cooper (so 35 released). Safest on screen: "every passenger". If spoken, "36 passengers" attributed to the FBI count.
- **The 66-volume FBI case file "went to the National Archives":** only in search summaries, not confirmed. If you show binders, don't state a number.
- **His quoted line "I have a grudge", the "cowboy passenger", the tie DNA and titanium-particle analysis, and Mount St. Helens destroying clues:** these come from secondary or contested accounts, aren't needed, and are left out.
- **Inflation-adjusted value** ("$1.6 million today"): an estimate (Wikipedia, 2025 dollars). Leave it out or say "well over a million dollars today" only if needed.
- **Real likenesses** of Cooper (composite sketches), the crew or Brian Ingram. No airline logos or FBI seals: use neutral minifigs and generic liveries.
- **Anything that reads as a how-to or glorifies hijacking:** frame the lesson as "copy the clarity, not the crime". Nobody was physically hurt (per accounts), but don't joke about the threat.
- **"Free"** in the CTA, and no App Store or URL read-out.
