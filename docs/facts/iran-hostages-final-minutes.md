## Brief
- **Category:** history story (a diplomatic showdown). Tactics: a third-party mediator, face-saving, and timing / deadlines.
- **Hook idea (spoken line 1, 13 words):** "He negotiated their freedom. The planes waited until he was no longer president."
  - The hook you asked for, *"He negotiated their freedom. His enemy made sure he'd never get the credit."*, **states a motive as fact** ("made sure"). Why Iran picked that moment is a historians' reading, not a stated fact. "His enemy" can also be heard as Reagan, which points at the disputed "October Surprise" theory. And "never get the credit" goes too far: historians do credit Carter's team, and Carter flew out to greet the hostages the next day. See "Do not say".
  - Other safe options: "He spent his last night freeing 52 hostages. They flew after noon." (12 words) · "He negotiated their freedom. The takeoff waited until he wasn't president." (11 words)
  - **Hook title** (works on mute and adds to the spoken line): **"DEADLINE: NOON"**. Alternative: **"444 DAYS. 30 MINUTES LATE."** This is safe because, by the sources below, the first plane left about 25 to 33 minutes after noon.
  - **Frame-0 visual:** a brick grandfather clock by an office door, its hands at 11:59. A desk phone's cord trails across the floor to a couch. Cut to a white jet on a dark runway, engines glowing, not moving.
- **Story arc (about 68 s):**
  1. **0–3 s, hook:** the clock, the stalled jet, the title card.
  2. **3–10 s, setup:** November 4, 1979. Students seize the US Embassy in Tehran, and Iran's revolutionary government backs them. 52 Americans end up held for 444 days. Ten days in, the US freezes Iran's money (Carter later put it at almost $12 billion).
  3. **10–17 s, the pressure:** April 1980: a rescue mission is called off, and eight US servicemen die in a collision. November 1980: Carter loses the election. Now there's a hard clock: at noon on January 20, he's no longer president.
  4. **17–29 s, "Move one: bring in a neutral":** Iran won't talk to Americans, so Algeria steps in between. Iran's side spoke Persian and Algeria's team spoke French, so by Carter's account every US proposal was translated twice on its way to Tehran. Even the final deal is written as Algeria's declaration, which each side signs up to separately.
  5. **29–41 s, "Move two: let both sides save face":** the US frames it as no ransom: Iran is getting its own money back. Iran's commitments are framed inside its parliament's four conditions. Neither side has to trust the other. The money sits in an escrow account at the Bank of England, in the name of Algeria's central bank. Algeria certifies the deposit, Iran lets the 52 go, and Algeria certifies they've left. Only then does the money move.
  6. **41–54 s, the last night:** Carter hasn't been to bed since early Sunday. He works the phones from the Oval Office, lying on a couch by the fireplace with the phones on the floor when things are slow. The telex from Iran's central bank comes through garbled, a few letters at a time. Christopher has already told the Algerians that US authority runs out at noon. About $8 billion moves to London, and the escrow papers are signed at 6:18 a.m. At 10:45, Rosalynn reminds him the Reagans arrive in 15 minutes.
  7. **54–62 s, the payoff:** at noon, Reagan is sworn in. The planes take off from Tehran at around 12:25 to 12:33 p.m. Washington time (sources differ). At lunch, Reagan announces it. **Hedge (keep it in the script):** "Many historians read the timing as a last snub to Carter. Others point to Iran needing the sanctions lifted for its war with Iraq." The next day, Carter flies to West Germany to greet them.
  8. **62–68 s:** the takeaway, the soft CTA, then the end-card hold.
- **Tactics (1–2):**
  1. **Use a trusted third party** when the two sides won't (or can't) talk. Algeria carried every message and held the money's keys, and the escrow did the trusting for both sides.
  2. **Face-saving and timing.** Give each side a version it can defend at home ("not a ransom" / "our parliament's conditions"). A deadline is leverage, but the other side can use the clock too.
- **Takeaway line:** "Give them a way to say yes without losing face, and watch who holds the clock." (Shorter: "A deadline is leverage. Remember: it ticks for both sides.")

## Facts
- On **November 4, 1979**, a group of Iranian students seized the US Embassy in Tehran and took its staff hostage. They cited the Shah's admission to the US for medical treatment (October 22, 1979). Khomeini's government approved the takeover. [NARA]
- On November 19–20, 1979, the students released 13 hostages: all of the women and the African Americans. Richard Queen was released on July 11, 1980 because of multiple sclerosis, "reducing the final number of U.S. hostages to 52". [NARA]
- Six US embassy employees who had avoided capture by hiding with the Canadians flew out of Tehran on January 28, 1980, using Canadian passports. [NARA]
- On **April 24, 1980**, a US rescue mission was called off after three of its eight helicopters failed. As the force pulled back, a helicopter collided with a C-130 transport plane, **killing eight US servicemen**. [NARA]
- Iraq invaded Iran on **September 22, 1980**, starting a war that lasted almost eight years. [NARA]
- Carter lost the presidential election on **November 4, 1980**. [NARA]
- Ten days after the hostages were taken, Carter froze Iranian assets held by Americans, including gold and bank deposits (the deal dates the freeze to November 14, 1979). Carter put the total at "almost $12 billion". A State Department statement put it at $11–12 billion. [Carter-TIME; Constable; GD]
- The final negotiations ran through Algeria. Iran's prime minister chose to negotiate through the Algerians, and from November 1980 the Algerian team carried each side's positions between Algiers, Washington and Tehran. Deputy Secretary of State **Warren Christopher** flew to Algiers on January 7, 1981. [Constable]
- Carter's account:
  - The Iranians "refused to communicate directly with me or any other American".
  - An Algerian team under Foreign Minister **Mohammed Benyahia** acted as go-between.
  - The Iranians spoke Persian and would talk only with the Algerians, who spoke French, so every US proposal "had to be translated twice". [Carter-TIME]
- The agreement is written as a **Declaration of the Government of Algeria**. Algeria, "having been requested by the Governments of the Islamic Republic of Iran and the United States of America to serve as an intermediary", declares the "interdependent commitments" each side made, "on the basis of formal adherences received from Iran and the United States". It is dated **January 19, 1981** (the Algiers Accords). [GD; NARA]
- The deal is explicitly framed "within the framework of the four points stated in the Resolution of November 2, 1980, of the Islamic Consultative Assembly of Iran" (Iran's parliament). [GD]
- The US aims, according to the State Department: get the hostages out safely and "avoid any element of ransom". The idea was to put Iran's finances back, as far as possible, to where they stood before the November 14, 1979 freeze. The US also pledged not to intervene in Iran's internal affairs. [Constable; GD]
- How the escrow worked (face-saving for both sides, with Algeria supplying the trust):
  - The escrow account was set up at the **Bank of England in the name of the Algerian Central Bank**. [Constable; GD]
  - On the morning of January 20, the Algerian Central Bank told the three governments that the deposit had arrived. That was Iran's signal to let the hostages leave safely. [Constable]
  - When the hostages had left Iranian airspace, Algeria certified that they were safely out. Only then was the escrow money released. [Constable; GD para. 3]
  - Either side could pull out on 72 hours' notice until Algeria made that certification. [GD]
- **The money:**
  - The US moved about **$7.955 billion** into escrow, according to the State Department statement. Carter's notes say the Bank of England certified **$7.977 billion**. Say "**about $8 billion**". [Constable; Carter-TIME]
  - After the release, about **$3.667 billion** paid off loans that groups of banks had made to Iran, about **$1.418 billion** stayed in escrow while disputes were settled, and about **$2.88 billion** went to Iran's central bank (Bank Markazi). [Constable]
  - Other claims went to binding arbitration (the Iran-United States Claims Tribunal), backed by a **$1 billion security account** that may not fall below $500 million. [GD; Constable]
- **Carter's last night (his own account):**
  - "I had not been to bed since early Sunday, and I was discouraged and almost exhausted." [Carter-TIME]
  - He worked from the Oval Office with Hamilton Jordan and Jody Powell, and Rosalynn kept urging him to rest. When things were slow he "lay down on one of the small couches before the fireplace, often with the telephones on the floor beside me." He "watched the big grandfather clock by the door." [Carter-TIME]
  - The afternoon before, Christopher was told to inform Benyahia that US authority "would expire at noon the next day", to press a deadline on the Algerians and the Iranians. [Carter-TIME]
  - The telex from Iran's central bank came through "garbled and confused; it came through a few letters at a time." At 3:16 a.m. came the report: "The money is moving to London." [Carter-TIME]
  - At 6:35 a.m., Christopher reported that all the escrow papers had been signed at 6:18 a.m. EST and that the Bank of England had certified $7.977 billion. The bank's certification was finished at 8:04, and Algeria confirmed it at 8:06. [Carter-TIME]
  - The planes sat at the end of the Tehran runway. Carter wrote that Iran delayed the takeoff "for no apparent rational purpose". At 9:45, Christopher said takeoff would come "before noon". It didn't. [Carter-TIME]
  - At 10:45, Rosalynn told him the Reagans would arrive in 15 minutes. He left the Oval Office "reluctantly". [Carter-TIME]
- **The takeoff:**
  - The 52 left Iran on **January 20, 1981**, "minutes after Ronald Reagan was sworn in as the 40th U.S. president". [NARA]
  - The takeoff time differs by source:
    - The NYT reported that two Boeing 727s took off at **12:25 p.m. EST**, "the very moment that Mr. Reagan was concluding his solemn Inaugural Address".
    - Carter wrote that he was told the first plane took off at **12:33 p.m.** and the second nine minutes later.
    - A State Department statement says "a few minutes after noon Washington time".
    - So say "**minutes after**" or "about half an hour after noon". [NYT via PolitiFact; Carter-TIME; Constable]
  - They flew on an Algerian plane (an Air Algérie Boeing 727) to Algiers. It was shortly after 9 p.m. in Tehran. In Algiers, Benyahia formally handed them over to Christopher. [Guardian-1981; Constable]
  - Reagan announced it at the inaugural luncheon in Statuary Hall at 2:15 p.m.: "Some 30 minutes ago, the planes bearing our prisoners left Iranian airspace and are now free of Iran." [Reagan-Lib]
  - Carter, back in Plains, Georgia, said he would fly to Wiesbaden, West Germany, to meet the hostages. He went the next day, **reportedly as Reagan's envoy**. I couldn't confirm from a primary source that Reagan asked him, so keep "reportedly" or drop that part. [Guardian-1981]
- **Why that timing? (Keep this hedged: it's interpretation, not fact.)**
  - Mark Bowden, who interviewed about 12 of the hostage-takers for *Guests of the Ayatollah*, believes the release was delayed "as a final thumb in the eye of Jimmy Carter". Historian David Farber says the Iranians waited "as a final insult to Carter, whom they despised". Tom Ahern, the CIA station chief and one of the hostages, said a captor told him they wouldn't get out "as long as Carter was president". [FactCheck; PolitiFact]
  - Political scientist David Houghton gives another reason: Iran needed the sanctions lifted to pay for its war with Iraq. FactCheck.org: "it is impossible for us to say with certainty what the hostage-takers were thinking." [PolitiFact; FactCheck]
  - Scholars told PolitiFact that Reagan played no significant role in the negotiations. [PolitiFact]

## Do not say
- **"His enemy made sure he'd never get the credit"** (the hook you asked for), or any line that states Iran's motive as fact ("Iran waited to humiliate Carter", "out of spite", "as revenge"). It's a historians' reading, so say "many historians read it as…". Also don't say he "never got the credit": Carter's team is widely credited, and he greeted the hostages in West Germany the next day.
- **The "October Surprise" theory**, that Reagan's 1980 campaign got Iran to delay the release. A 1993 House task force found no credible evidence. A 2023 account by Ben Barnes about John Connally revived it, but it's still unproven. Leave it out entirely, and don't let "his enemy" point at Reagan.
- **"Reagan freed the hostages" / "Iran feared Reagan".** Scholars say Reagan played no significant role (PolitiFact rated the claim "Pants on Fire").
- **"The US paid Iran an $8 billion ransom."** The money was Iran's own frozen assets, and only about $2.88 billion went straight to Iran that day. Most of the rest paid off bank loans or stayed in escrow. Say "about $8 billion of Iran's frozen money moved into escrow".
- **An exact takeoff minute** (12:25 vs 12:33 vs "a few minutes after noon"). Say "minutes after" or "about half an hour after noon".
- **The exact minute of the oath** (not checked). Say "at noon" for the handover of power (the constitutional deadline) and "minutes after he was sworn in".
- **"Carter didn't sleep for X days."** Use only his own line: he hadn't been to bed since early Sunday.
- **"Reagan asked Carter to go to Wiesbaden"** as a flat fact. It's widely reported, but I couldn't confirm it from a primary source. Say "reportedly", or just "Carter flew to West Germany to greet them".
- **Who first came up with writing the deal as Algeria's declaration.** Secondary sources credit the Algerian mediators, but no primary source confirms it. Say only that the deal was "written as Algeria's declaration".
- **Details of how the hostages were treated** (mock executions, beatings, names). Not checked this time, and not needed.
- **Compensation for the hostages, later lawsuits, or the Claims Tribunal's later awards.** Not checked this time.
- **The White House Christmas tree lights** being switched on after the release. This came only from a search summary and wasn't confirmed, so leave it out.
- **"66 hostages"** as a spoken number. It follows from 52 + 13 + 1, but some of those held were at Iran's Foreign Ministry, not the embassy. Say only "52 Americans".
- **Real emblems, seals, flags with emblems, airline logos, or a Khomeini minifig** in 3D. Signs read `EMBASSY`, `ESCROW`, `BANK`, `ALGIERS`, `NOON`.
- **Statistics about how often mediation succeeds.** None were checked, and none may be made up.
