# lima-embassy-tunnels: For 126 days they talked, and someone was digging under the floor

## Brief
- **Category:** (1) History story: a hostage standoff, Lima, Peru, Dec 1996 – Apr 1997.
- **Hook idea (≤ 14 words, spoken line 1):** "For 126 days they negotiated. The whole time, someone was digging under the floor." (14 words)
  - Mute-safe hook title that adds to the line: **"THE TALKS WERE COVER"**. Visual from frame 0: a brick floor cutaway, with negotiators at a table above and a lit tunnel creeping toward them below.
- **Story arc (60–75 s):**
  1. **0–3 s, hook:** split cutaway: a talks table above, miners' lamps below.
  2. **3–12 s, the party:** Dec 17, 1996. A birthday reception for the Emperor at the Japanese ambassador's residence in Lima. 14 MRTA rebels get in by blasting through a garden wall from the house next door, and hundreds of guests become hostages.
  3. **12–22 s, the demand:** release about 400 jailed comrades. President Fujimori refuses. Most hostages are let go over the following days and weeks, and **72** stay inside.
  4. **22–35 s, the talks:** a guarantors' commission (an archbishop, the Red Cross, Canada's ambassador). From February, face-to-face talks run in a house across the street. **Move one: keep them talking.** Each session buys time.
  5. **35–45 s, the dig:** under the talks, miners tunnel toward the house. Commandos rehearse on a full-size replica. Loud music and marches cover the noise (hedge: "reportedly"). **The tell:** in March, the rebel leader hears noises underground, accuses the government of digging and breaks off the talks. The digging goes on.
  6. **45–55 s, the payoff:** April 22, 1997, about 3:20 p.m. The rebels are playing indoor soccer in the main hall. Charges go off under the floor and about 140 commandos come up through it. 71 of the 72 hostages are rescued. One hostage, two commandos and all 14 rebels die.
  7. **55–62 s, the hedge (one line, spoken):** "Years later, a regional human-rights court ruled that at least one rebel was executed after he was captured."
  8. **62–70 s, takeaway + soft CTA.**
- **Tactics (1–2):**
  1. **Talks buy time:** crisis-negotiation doctrine (FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin / CNU) holds that time works for the negotiator. It lets emotions cool and gives the other side time to gather intelligence and plan.
  2. **How to read a stall:** if talks drag on with no movement on the core issue, ask what the other side is doing with the time. Here the hard "no" on prisoners never moved, and that was the tell.
- **One-line takeaway:** "When talks drag on with no movement, ask what the time is buying them."

## Facts
- On **December 17, 1996**, members of the Túpac Amaru Revolutionary Movement (MRTA) seized the official residence of Japan's ambassador in Lima during a reception for Emperor Akihito's birthday. [Wikipedia "Japanese embassy hostage crisis"; ADST oral history; TIME 1996–97 coverage]
- There were **14** attackers, led by **Néstor Cerpa Cartolini**. [Wikipedia; History.com; IACtHR *Cruz Sánchez et al. v. Peru* background]
- The host was Ambassador **Morihisa Aoki**. The residence is in Lima's San Isidro district. [Wikipedia; TIME]
- **Hundreds** of guests were taken hostage: diplomats, government and military officials and business executives (reported counts vary, roughly 400–800; say "hundreds"). [Wikipedia; ADST; IACtHR summary]
- The rebels reportedly got in by blowing a hole in a wall from a neighbouring property. [Wikipedia; TIME]
- The MRTA demanded the release of about **400** imprisoned members, Cerpa's wife among them. Fujimori refused to free them. [History.com; ADST; TIME]
- Most hostages were released in the days and weeks after the takeover. **72** were held until the end, and the standoff lasted **126 days**. [Wikipedia; ADST]
- A guarantors' commission mediated: Archbishop **Juan Luis Cipriani**, ICRC delegate **Michel Minnig** and Canadian ambassador **Anthony Vincent**. [Washington Post 1997; UNM / GWU National Security Archive materials]
- Around **Feb 1, 1997**, Fujimori met Japanese PM **Ryutaro Hashimoto** in **Toronto**. Japan urged a peaceful solution. [UNM; Univ. of Delaware chronology; Washington Post]
- Face-to-face talks between government negotiator **Domingo Palermo** (Education Minister) and **Cerpa** began around **Feb 11, 1997**, in a house across the street from the residence. [Washington Post 1997]
- Meanwhile the government secretly prepared a military option: tunnels were dug under the residence. Accounts say miners did the digging. [Washington Post 1997; TIME; Canadian Encyclopedia]
- Commandos rehearsed the assault on a full-scale replica of the residence. [Wikipedia; Washington Post]
- The authorities reportedly masked the digging noise with loud music, military marches and vehicle movement around the perimeter. (Keep "reportedly".) [ADST oral history]
- In early **March 1997** (reported as about March 6), Cerpa suspended the talks, saying noises under the floor showed the government was digging tunnels. The government played this down, but the digging went on. [GWU National Security Archive; Washington Post; AP via Cayman Compass]
- Hostages inside reportedly helped the outside track the rebels, using hidden microphones and transmitters smuggled into the house. Vice Admiral **Luis Giampietri**, a hostage, is reported to have played a key role. (Keep "reportedly".) [Wikipedia "Operation Chavín de Huántar"; TIME / Washington Post]
- On **April 22, 1997**, Fujimori ordered **Operation Chavín de Huántar**. At about **3:20 p.m.**, while several rebels, Cerpa among them, were playing **indoor soccer** in the ground-floor hall, explosives went off beneath the floor and commandos came up from the tunnels. [Canadian Encyclopedia; LA Times; TIME; Washington Post]
- About **140** commandos took part. [Canadian Encyclopedia; Washington Post]
- **71 of the 72** hostages were rescued. One hostage, Supreme Court justice **Carlos Giusti**, died, as did two commandos (**Juan Valer** and **Raúl Jiménez**). **All 14 rebels** were killed. [Wikipedia; LA Times; Peruvian Armed Forces (ccffaa.mil.pe)]
- Hedge: Japanese diplomat **Hidetaka Ogura**, a former hostage, later testified (from about 2000–2001) that he saw some rebels **alive and in custody** after the raid. This led to allegations that rebels were executed after surrendering. [OAS / IACHR documents; PUCP; El Comercio]
- In ***Cruz Sánchez et al. v. Peru*** (judgment dated **April 17, 2015**, made public June 2015), the **Inter-American Court of Human Rights** held Peru responsible for the **extrajudicial execution of one rebel, Eduardo Nicolás Cruz Sánchez ("Tito")**. It found **insufficient evidence** for two other alleged cases. It did not question the legality of the rescue operation as a whole. [IACtHR judgment; CEJIL; JusticeInfo; Peru Support Group]
- Tactic source: FBI crisis-negotiation doctrine (FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin / CNU) treats time as working in the negotiator's favour. Slowing down lowers emotional intensity and gives the other side time to gather intelligence and plan. [FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin articles, fbi.gov]

## Do not say
- **Any exact guest count** ("600", "700", "800", "over 1,000 invited"): figures vary by source. Say "hundreds".
- **That the rebels posed as waiters**: a popular claim, but the documented entry is through a wall from a neighbouring house.
- **Exact tunnel length, number of tunnels or number of miners** ("200 yards", "24–60 miners", "carpeted tunnels"): accounts differ.
- **The code phrase "Mary está enferma", the "bugged guitar", Bibles or thermoses**: reported, but details vary. Say only "hidden microphones, reportedly".
- **That the raid took exactly X minutes** ("16 minutes", "15–20 minutes"): sources vary. "Minutes" at most.
- **That several or all of the rebels were executed**: only the IACtHR finding for **one** rebel (Cruz Sánchez) is established. Others are allegations the court found unproven. Never call it "bloodless" either.
- **That the rebels played soccer "every day at exactly 3 p.m."**: say only that they were playing indoor soccer when the raid began.
- **Which Fujimori family members were held, and whether any were among the final 72**: sources conflict. Leave out.
- **That the talks were deliberately a sham from day one**: not established. Say that the talks went on while the tunnels were dug, and that the core demand never moved.
- **Any statistic on how often stalling tactics work, or on hostage survival rates**: none was verified. Use only the FBI doctrine principle.
- **Fujimori's later convictions** (unrelated to this raid): off-topic. Leave out.
