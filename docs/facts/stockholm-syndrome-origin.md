## Brief
- **Category:** History story (a hostage standoff / negotiation under pressure), Stockholm, 23–28 Aug 1973.
- **Hook idea (spoken line 1, 13 words):** "A hostage begged the Prime Minister to let her leave… with the robbers."
  - Alt (your line, narrowed to one hostage): "One hostage was more afraid of the police than of the robbers."
  - **Hook title (works on mute, adds rather than repeats):** "SHE TRUSTED THE GUNMEN" (or "STOCKHOLM, 1973: SHE TRUSTED THE GUNMEN").
  - **Visual from frame 0:** brick vault door half open; a young bank-clerk minifig with a rotary-phone cord running out of the vault; police helmets silhouetted outside.
- **Story arc (about 62–68 s of voice):**
  1. **0–3 s, hook:** the phone call from inside the vault.
  2. **3–12 s:** 23 Aug 1973, Kreditbanken on Norrmalmstorg square. A convict who hadn't gone back to prison after a furlough fires a submachine gun into the ceiling and takes four bank employees hostage.
  3. **12–22 s, the strange demand:** money, guns, bulletproof vests, helmets, a getaway car… and his convict friend Clark Olofsson, brought from prison. The government says yes and Olofsson is driven to the bank. The robbers can have a car, but **they may not take the hostages with them**.
  4. **22–36 s, six days in the vault:** the robber puts his jacket over a shivering hostage. Police drill holes in the vault ceiling from above. Hostage Kristin Enmark phones Prime Minister Olof Palme: "I fully trust Clark and the robber… what I am scared of is that the police will attack and cause us to die." She asks to leave **in the car, with them**. Palme refuses.
  5. **36–45 s, the end:** 28 Aug, police pump tear gas into the vault and the robbers surrender. The hostages won't walk out first: "No, Jan and Clark go first. You'll gun them down if we do!"
  6. **45–58 s, the twist:** a psychiatrist advising the police names it the "Norrmalmstorg syndrome", later known worldwide as Stockholm syndrome. He spent the week at the bank but, by most accounts, **never interviewed the hostages**. It has never been in the DSM, and an FBI review found 92% of hostage victims showed no signs of it.
  7. **58–66 s:** takeaway, then the soft CTA.
- **Tactics (1–2):**
  - **Find out who they think the threat is.** The hostages weren't irrational: they sided with whoever looked likelier to keep them alive. In any negotiation, work out what the other side fears before you push (tactical empathy, from FBI crisis negotiation).
  - **The label as a tactic.** Calling someone's objection a "syndrome" (or "emotional") lets you skip answering it. If someone labels you, go back to the facts. If you're tempted to label someone, first ask what they're afraid of.
- **Takeaway (one line):** "Before you call someone irrational, ask what they're afraid of."

## Facts
- **When and where:** 23–28 August 1973, at Kreditbanken (Sveriges Kreditbank) on Norrmalmstorg square in central Stockholm. [History.com, "Stockholm Syndrome: The True Story of Hostages Loyal to Their Captor"; Wikipedia, "Norrmalmstorg robbery"]
- **The robber:** Jan-Erik Olsson, a safecracker who had failed to return to prison after a furlough from a three-year sentence for grand larceny. On the morning of 23 Aug he pulled a loaded submachine gun from under a folded jacket and fired at the ceiling. Reportedly disguising his voice to sound American, he shouted in English, "The party has just begun!" [History.com]
- He wounded a police officer (Ingemar Warpefeldt, shot in the hand) who had responded to the alarm. [History.com; Wikipedia]
- **Four hostages, all bank employees:** Birgitta Lundblad, Elisabeth Oldgren, Kristin Enmark (23) and Sven Säfström (three women, one man). Säfström had reportedly hidden in the bank and was found later. [Wikipedia EN; Swedish Wikipedia, "Norrmalmstorgsdramat"; History.com]
- **Demands:** his friend Clark Olofsson brought to the bank, plus 3 million Swedish kronor (History.com: "more than $700,000 in Swedish and foreign currency"), two guns, bulletproof vests, helmets and a getaway car (a Ford Mustang). [Wikipedia; History.com]
- **The government brought the convict friend from prison.** Olofsson was serving a sentence for armed robbery. By government decision he was fetched from prison (in Norrköping, per Swedish Wikipedia) and let into the bank; Justice Minister Lennart Geijer allowed it. Officials reportedly hoped he would act as a link to the police negotiators or calm things down. Instead he joined Olsson. [Wikipedia EN + SV; History.com]
- **Within hours,** police delivered Olofsson, the ransom and a blue Ford Mustang with a full tank, **but refused to let the robbers leave with the hostages.** [History.com]
- The robbers and hostages barricaded themselves in the bank's vault. [Wikipedia; History.com]
- **Kindness inside:** Olsson put a wool jacket over Enmark's shoulders when she shivered and soothed her after a bad dream. He let Oldgren, who felt claustrophobic, walk outside the vault on a 30-foot rope. Säfström later said: "When he treated us well, we could think of him as an emergency God." [History.com]
- **There were threats too (keep the balance):** Olsson threatened to kill hostages and once held one in a stranglehold while on the phone with Palme. When police first tried gas, the hostages were made to stand with nooses around their necks. [Wikipedia; Nils Bejerot, "The six day war in Stockholm", New Scientist, 1974]
- Olsson threatened to shoot Säfström in the leg to shake up the police. Säfström later recalled "how kind I thought he was for saying it was just my leg." [History.com]
- **The call to the Prime Minister:** Enmark phoned Prime Minister Olof Palme and pleaded with him to let the robbers take her with them in the escape car: "I fully trust Clark and the robber. I am not desperate. They haven't done a thing to us. On the contrary, they have been very nice. But, you know, Olof, what I am scared of is that the police will attack and cause us to die." [History.com; Wikipedia: she "asked him to let the robbers and the hostages leave"]
- **Palme refused.** Swedish Wikipedia paraphrases his reason as not wanting to "let such people loose on the roads". A recording of Enmark's call with Palme survives. [Swedish Wikipedia]
- **"More afraid of the police":** in a radio interview during the siege (Sveriges Radio's Ekot), Enmark said, "I'm not the least bit afraid of Clark and the other guy. I'm afraid of the police." By the second day the hostages reportedly feared the police more than their captors. [Swedish Wikipedia (Ekot quote); History.com]
- **Followed live:** the siege is described as the first crime in Sweden covered by live television. Press photographers and police snipers lay side by side on a roof opposite the bank. [Wikipedia EN; History.com photo caption, 24 Aug 1973]
- **Drilling:** on 26 Aug police drilled into the vault from above and took a widely circulated photo of the hostages with Olofsson. Olsson fired into the holes and wounded a police officer in the hand and face. [Wikipedia EN + SV]
- **The end:** on the night of 28 Aug, after more than 130 hours, police pumped tear gas into the vault and the robbers surrendered (Wikipedia: "after an hour"; History.com: "quickly"). None of the hostages was permanently injured. [History.com; Wikipedia]
- **The hostages wouldn't come out first.** Enmark yelled, "No, Jan and Clark go first. You'll gun them down if we do!" Two of the women cried, "Don't hurt them, they didn't harm us." [History.com]
- **Afterwards:** Olsson got 10 years. Olofsson was convicted at first, then acquitted by the Svea Court of Appeal; he said he had only tried to keep the hostages calm. Olofsson and Enmark met several times later, and their families became friends. [Wikipedia EN + SV; History.com]
- **Who coined the term:** Nils Bejerot, a Swedish psychiatrist and criminologist who was consultant psychiatrist to the police. He wrote that he spent the whole week at the bank. He called it Norrmalmstorgssyndromet (the Norrmalmstorg syndrome); outside Sweden it became "Stockholm syndrome". [Bejerot, New Scientist 1974; Wikipedia, "Stockholm syndrome"]
- **The twist (hedged):** Bejerot reportedly never met, spoke to or interviewed the hostages, during or after the siege. Even so, he described their reactions as the result of being "brainwashed". Journalist Jess Hill wrote that his diagnosis of Enmark was made "without ever having spoken to her". [Wikipedia, "Stockholm syndrome" (citing Adorjan et al., The Sociological Quarterly, 2012); Jess Hill, See What You Made Me Do, 2019]
  - Script wording: "He never interviewed them." Do **not** say he wasn't there: he was at the bank, and police listened to the vault through microphones. [Bejerot, New Scientist 1974]
- **Enmark's own view:** for decades she maintained that she felt no affinity for her captors and did what it took to stay alive, and that the authorities' handling put the hostages in danger. [Wikipedia, "Stockholm syndrome" (citing Jess Hill)] Her memoir is Jag blev Stockholmssyndromet ("I Became the Stockholm Syndrome", 2015, with Gunnar Wesslén).
- **Not an official diagnosis:** Stockholm syndrome has never been in the DSM. [Wikipedia, "Stockholm syndrome" (Adorjan et al. 2012); Cleveland Clinic]
- **It's rare:** G. Dwayne Fuselier's article "Placing the Stockholm Syndrome in Perspective" (FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin, July 1999) used the FBI's Hostage/Barricade database of over 1,200 incidents. In that data, 92% of victims showed no signs of Stockholm syndrome. [FBI LEB, July 1999]
- **Tactic sources:** labelling and tactical empathy come from FBI crisis-negotiation practice (the Behavioral Change Stairway Model; Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference, 2016). [See docs/facts/fbi-negotiation.md]

## Do not say
- **That the hostages refused to testify against the robbers, or raised money for their defence.** It's often repeated, but I found no solid primary source. Wikipedia notes that many popular details of this case are partly or entirely made up.
- **That a hostage married or got engaged to a robber.** False: Wikipedia says Olsson's later fiancée was not a former hostage. Enmark and Olofsson only became family friends.
- **Palme's reported "Wouldn't it be nice to die at your post?"** Enmark, Olsson, Olofsson and a police official claimed this in 2003. They said it was on a 20-minute stretch of the tape that was erased, so it is unrecorded and disputed.
- **"Palme hung up on her" or any exact Palme wording:** not verified.
- **"You're playing checkers with our lives":** quoted only by secondary and tabloid sources. Use the History.com quote instead.
- **The police sending a misidentified 16-year-old boy into the bank:** comes from one secondary account (via Jess Hill / Wikipedia) and isn't confirmed elsewhere.
- **"It was first called Kramfors syndrome":** wrong. It was Norrmalmstorgssyndromet.
- **That Bejerot invented the term to cover up police mistakes.** That is the critics' reading (Allan Wade, Jess Hill). Use it only as "critics argue…", or skip it.
- **"Bejerot was never at the scene":** false. He was at the bank all week.
- **Unverified or anecdotal details:** Olsson's exact explosives, the money Olofsson reportedly smuggled out, Olsson's wig or disguise.
- **"All the hostages loved their captors":** too broad. Use Enmark's own words and the History.com anecdotes.
- **Any statistic other than the FBI's 1999 figure of 92%:** don't invent numbers.
- **Visual safety:** no gun pointed at or fired at a person on screen; no nooses (use a coiled-rope prop); no realistic gas injuries. Show tear gas as white plumes of 1×1 studs.
