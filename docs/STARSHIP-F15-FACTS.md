## Naming note
The person asked for "Flight 13, November 2026". **Flight 13 flew on July 24, 2026** (suborbital, no ship catch, booster lost in the water, ship splashed down in the Indian Ocean).
Every event the person listed (liftoff → booster water landing with termination → orbit → Starlink deploy → orbits → reentry → belly flip → ship caught by the tower) happened on **Flight 15, launched November 13, 2026**. All four reference videos are about Flight 15. The videos say Flight 15.

## Vehicles and site
- Booster 22 (Super Heavy, Block 3) and Ship 42 (Block 3). Ship 42 carries the words "Don't Panic" in addition to its number.
- Launched from Starbase, Texas, **Orbital Launch Pad 2 (Pad 2)**, next to Boca Chica beach. Pad 2 has a flame trench.
- Block 3 Super Heavy: 33 Raptor engines (3 centre, 10 middle ring, 20 outer ring), 3 grid fins, integrated vented hot-stage ring.
- Ship: 6 Raptors (3 sea-level, 3 vacuum), 4 flaps (2 forward, 2 aft), black hexagonal heat-shield tiles on the windward side.

## Why the booster missed (as stated, attributed)
- SpaceX spokesperson Dan Huot: it was not a GNC fault; GNC triggered the abort and "was doing what it was supposed to do". Root cause still under investigation at the time.
- Elon Musk: the engines slightly underperformed, leaving too little propellant; the fix is to cut MECO about one second earlier.

## Names
- "Mechazilla" is the widely used nickname (coined by Elon Musk) for the launch tower with the catch arms ("chopsticks").
- Boosters had been caught by a tower before (Flights 5, 7, 8 and others); Flight 15 attempted the first catch of BOTH stages, and caught the ship only. Say "catch both on one flight".
- Elon Musk's explanation and fix were given while SpaceX said the root cause was still under investigation: attribute them to Musk.

## Weather/look
- Launch day: clear, late-afternoon sun, big white steam clouds at liftoff.
- Catch day: overcast grey sky over the tidal flats.
