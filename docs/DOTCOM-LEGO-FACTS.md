# dotcom-crash-lego: "Add .com, get rich?" in LEGO (brief + fact sheet)

A LEGO / brick video-game remake of `dotcom-crash` (the original was deliberately made in a 2D cartoon style as the
workspace's cartoon reference). Made by `builder-legoCouncil`, judged by the 6-seat legoCouncil (Claude Opus 5.5,
screenshots only). This is **not** a DealCalm video: there is no DealCalm CTA.

## Brief
- **Length:** about 95 s (voice 90–100 s plus the 2.4 s end hold). Vertical 1080x1920, 30 fps.
- **Voice:** `en-US-Chirp3-HD-Enceladus` (the person chose it). It is a slow voice: rate 1.2 brought the 58 s cartoon script to about 57.6 s, so start at rate about 1.2 and adjust to land the length.
- **Story arc:** the mania (1998–99) → Pets.com as the poster child → the peak (March 10, 2000) → the Fed and the turn → the crash (2000–2002) → the survivors and the long climb back → the lesson.
- **Hook idea** (≤ 14 words; rewrite freely): "In 1999, adding dot com to your name could make your stock jump."
- **Ending:** the line "So when everyone says this time is different, remember the sock puppet." (or a tighter version), then a subtle follow pull. No sales pitch.
- **Props quality bar:** `examples/disney-iger-r7/` (use `assets/props-kit.js`: ≥ 6 asset types per set, ≥ 3 unique to that set, extras holding props, live props).
- **Sets (8+, fully dressed, brick-built, late-90s flavour):**
  - a 1999 office with beige CRT monitors and a "BOB'S SHOES → BOB'S SHOES.COM" sign change;
  - a trading floor with ticker boards (brick tiles), phones and traders;
  - an IPO / stock-exchange bell podium with confetti;
  - a pet-food warehouse with pallets of heavy bags and loss-making price tags;
  - a delivery-van street (free shipping) with bags piling up;
  - a Super Bowl-style stadium TV ad with a brick sock-puppet mascot (generic sock puppet; no real logo);
  - a Federal Reserve-style marble building with a rate board ticking up;
  - an empty, foreclosed startup office (bean bags, scooters, "CLOSED" sign), and a grocery-van lot for Webvan;
  - a Nasdaq "mountain" of brick bars rising to the peak and collapsing;
  - a survivors skyline (generic brick towers; no Amazon logo) and a slow climb back to 2015.
- **People:** generic investors, traders, founders and a sock puppet. No real people need to appear. Company names may be spoken and shown in captions and HUD tags; 3D signs show generic words only (no Pets.com, Amazon, Nasdaq or toy-brand logos; "PETS", "GROCERIES", "EXCHANGE" are fine).

## Facts (the ONLY facts the script may state)
- In the late 1990s, adding ".com" to a company's name could make its stock jump; investors poured money into internet companies with little or no profit.
- November 1998: theglobe.com's stock rose about 606% on its first day of trading.
- 1999: the Nasdaq rose about 85.6% for the year (say "about eighty-five percent").
- Pets.com sold pet food for less than it paid for it, and shipped heavy bags for free. It had a famous sock-puppet mascot and a Super Bowl ad. It went public in February 2000 at $11 a share, and shut down in November 2000.
- March 10, 2000: the Nasdaq peaked at 5,048.62 (say "above five thousand").
- From June 1999 to May 2000 the Federal Reserve raised interest rates six times; cheap money dried up, and investors started asking where the profits were.
- Webvan, the grocery-delivery startup, went bankrupt in July 2001.
- Amazon's stock fell about 94% from its peak (say "more than ninety percent").
- October 9, 2002: the Nasdaq bottomed at about 1,114, down about 78% from the peak.
- The Nasdaq didn't close above its 2000 peak again until April 23, 2015, about fifteen years later.
- The internet didn't die; the hype did. Survivors like Amazon became giants.
